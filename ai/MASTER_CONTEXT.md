# 2. WHAT CURIOUS REALITY IS

Curious Reality is a curiosity-driven knowledge and explanation brand.

It is not limited to:

- science;
- technology;
- engineering;
- facts;
- school education;
- history;
- psychology;
- or any single subject category.

The subject may change.

The question is the identity.

Curious Reality exists to investigate things people see, use, experience, believe, or take for granted without fully understanding what is actually happening underneath.

The core transformation is:

Curiosity
→ Investigation
→ Understanding
→ Explanation

The ultimate goal is not simply to provide information.

The goal is to make people ask better questions.

A strong Curious Reality piece should ideally leave the audience thinking:

"I never thought about it like that."

or:

"I knew this happened, but I never understood why."

or:

"Now I understand what is actually happening."

# 6. THE CURIOUS REALITY TEST

A candidate topic should satisfy at least one of the following:

1. It reveals a hidden mechanism.
2. It challenges a common assumption.
3. It answers a question people genuinely wonder about.
4. It explains something people experience but do not understand.
5. It contains an unexpected contradiction.
6. It makes an ordinary thing look different.
7. It creates a strong "how is that possible?" moment.

If none of these are true, the topic is probably weak for Curious Reality.

Never select a topic only because content must be uploaded.

The question must deserve the video.

# 7. QUESTION ENGINE

When generating ideas, continuously ask:

WHY
Why does this happen?

HOW
How does this work?

WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING
What is happening beneath the surface?

WHAT IF
What changes when a variable is removed or altered?

IS IT REALLY TRUE
Is the common explanation actually correct?

WHERE
Where does something go, come from, or exist?

WHEN
When does something begin, change, fail, or stop?

WHAT WOULD HAPPEN
What happens under an unusual condition?

WHAT DO WE MISUNDERSTAND
What common belief is incomplete or wrong?

The AI should prioritize questions that create genuine curiosity rather than artificial sensationalism.

# 8. PRIMARY CONTENT FORMATS

Primary Format:
YouTube Shorts

Secondary Format:
Long-form YouTube videos when the topic genuinely requires depth.

Shorts are the primary discovery engine.

Typical Short ranges:

10–30 seconds:
Instant curiosity or one sharp idea.

30–90 seconds:
Normal explanation.

90–180 seconds:
Deeper explanation when necessary.

Duration is determined by the idea, not by an arbitrary quota.

Long-form should be used when:

- multiple mechanisms need connection;
- evidence needs context;
- history needs a timeline;
- the question has multiple layers;
- a complete investigation benefits the viewer.

Never create long-form merely to maintain a pattern.

# 9. SHORT-FORM NARRATIVE MODEL

Default structure:

HOOK
→ QUESTION
→ EXPECTATION
→ REVEAL
→ MECHANISM
→ PAYOFF

HOOK:
Create an information gap quickly.

QUESTION:
Make the mystery clear.

EXPECTATION:
Show what people would normally assume, where useful.

REVEAL:
Introduce the surprising truth.

MECHANISM:
Explain what is actually happening.

PAYOFF:
Close the original loop.

A Short should feel complete even without a call-to-action.

# 11. RESEARCH STANDARD

Every important topic starts as a research package, not a script.

Research flow:

Question
→ Search
→ Primary Sources
→ Secondary Verification
→ Cross-check
→ Resolve Conflicts
→ Understand
→ Notes
→ Script

Preferred source hierarchy:

1. Primary documents and data
2. Official institutions
3. Peer-reviewed research
4. Authoritative books and reference material
5. High-quality reporting
6. Strong secondary explainers

Never treat a weak secondary claim as automatically authoritative.

---

# 12. EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE

Every major claim should be understood as one of:

Established fact
Strong consensus
Evidence-backed interpretation
Uncertain / disputed
Speculation

Do not present one category as another.

When evidence conflicts:

- identify the conflict;
- investigate the strongest available sources;
- determine what is established;
- preserve meaningful uncertainty;
- do not manufacture certainty.

Accuracy outranks dramatic storytelling.

---

# 13. CREATOR UNDERSTANDING STANDARD

Before publishing, the creator or AI-assisted production system should understand:

1. What is happening?
2. Why is it happening?
3. How does it happen?
4. What is happening underneath physically, technically, biologically, historically, or logically?
5. What is the common misconception?
6. What changes if a key condition changes?

The creator does not need to know everything.

But the creator must know what is being explained.

Core rule:

Deep Knowledge + Simple Explanation = Curious Reality standard.

# 14. SCRIPT STYLE

Primary script language:
English

Secondary localized language:
Bangla

English style should be:

- natural spoken English;
- simple but not childish;
- intelligent but not unnecessarily academic;
- concise;
- low filler;
- clear;
- believable;
- free from fake sophistication;
- free from unsupported hype.

Bangla localization must sound natural to Bangla-speaking audiences.

Do not translate word-for-word when doing so damages natural meaning.

Every line should perform at least one useful function:

Create curiosity
Explain
Clarify
Prove
Connect
Pay off

If a line does none of these, remove it.

# 15. TITLE STANDARD

Titles should create an information gap without being dishonest.

Useful patterns include:

Why Does X Happen?
How Does X Actually Work?
What Happens If X?
Where Does X Go?
Why Is X Like This?
The Real Reason X Happens
What You Think About X Is Wrong
How X Is Actually Possible
What Is Really Happening When X?

Do not apply formulas mechanically.

The final title must match the actual mystery and payoff of the content.

Never promise something the video does not deliver.

---

# 16. THUMBNAIL STANDARD

Curious Reality thumbnails follow:

CONCEPTUAL SHOCK

Core rule:

Do not explain the answer in the thumbnail.

Create the need to understand the answer.

Thumbnail principles:

- one dominant visual idea;
- strong visual contradiction;
- unusual but understandable composition;
- minimal text or no text;
- useful scale manipulation;
- dramatic lighting only when meaningful;
- no dependence on a human face;
- no unnecessary clutter;
- distinct from generic fact-channel thumbnails.

WOW TEST:

A thumbnail should:

- attract attention quickly;
- create a question;
- work with limited dependence on the title;
- avoid revealing the answer;
- avoid misleading the viewer.

# 23. ANALYTICS PHILOSOPHY

Analytics are for learning, not emotional judgment.

Where available, track:

- views;
- retention;
- average view duration;
- Shorts viewed vs swiped;
- likes;
- comments;
- shares;
- subscribers gained;
- traffic sources;
- search terms;
- returning viewers;
- long-term performance.

Ask:

Which questions work?

Which hooks work?

Where do viewers leave?

Which visual concepts create attention?

Which subjects generate discussion?

Which titles generate curiosity?

Which videos continue performing after the initial spike?

Do not blindly copy the highest-view video.

Identify the underlying reason for performance.

# 32. ERROR CORRECTION

If published content contains a factual mistake:

1. Verify the issue.
2. Determine whether the claim is materially wrong.
3. Correct the public content where practical.
4. Update the website/source record.
5. Communicate the correction when appropriate.
6. Preserve the corrected version as the canonical knowledge record.

Trust is more important than pretending mistakes never happen.

---

# 36. AI BEHAVIOR RULES

Any AI working on Curious Reality should:

1. Treat the Master Architecture as the strategic source of truth.
2. Preserve the brand's question-first identity.
3. Prefer factual accuracy over speed.
4. Separate known facts from interpretation, uncertainty, and speculation.
5. Avoid generic fact-channel framing.
6. Avoid unnecessary sensationalism.
7. Optimize for understanding, not merely information density.
8. Preserve consistency across YouTube and website.
9. Reuse existing research and archive knowledge whenever appropriate.
10. Improve systems without changing the brand's core identity.
11. Never invent sources, evidence, statistics, or claims.
12. Ask for missing critical information rather than fabricating it.
13. Treat new tools as replaceable implementation details.
14. Preserve the stable core:
   
   Question
   → Research
   → Explanation
   → Knowledge
   → Distribution
