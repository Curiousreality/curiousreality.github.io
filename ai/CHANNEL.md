# 2. CHANNEL PURPOSE

Curious Reality exists to investigate questions hidden inside everyday life, science, technology, engineering, history, psychology, space, human behavior, systems, machines, and other areas of reality.

The channel should make viewers understand something they previously:

- never questioned;
- misunderstood;
- took for granted;
- found strange;
- or could not explain.

The channel is therefore not organized around one fixed niche.

Its stable identity is:

QUESTIONS.

---

# 3. CHANNEL MISSION

Turn curiosity into understanding.

The channel operational loop is:

QUESTION
→ RESEARCH
→ VERIFICATION
→ UNDERSTANDING
→ EXPLANATION
→ VISUAL STORY
→ PUBLICATION
→ LEARNING
→ ARCHIVE

Every major upload should pass through this loop.

# 4. PRIMARY FORMAT

Primary format:

YouTube Shorts

Shorts are the main discovery engine for Curious Reality.

Typical use:

- one sharp question;
- one surprising mechanism;
- one counterintuitive fact;
- one misunderstood phenomenon;
- one compact explanation;
- one unusual observation.

Typical duration ranges:

10–30 sec:
Instant curiosity / one sharp idea.

30–90 sec:
Normal explanation.

90–180 sec:
Deeper explanation where the subject requires more context.

Duration must follow the idea.

Do not artificially stretch an idea.

Do not unnecessarily compress an idea.

---

# 5. SECONDARY FORMAT

Secondary format:

Long-form YouTube videos.

Long-form should only be produced when the subject genuinely requires depth.

Long-form is appropriate when:

- several mechanisms need to be connected;
- evidence requires context;
- historical development matters;
- the question has multiple layers;
- contradictions need investigation;
- the audience benefits from a complete explanation;
- a subject cannot be responsibly explained in a Short.

Never create a long-form video simply to maintain an upload pattern.

# 7. CHANNEL CONTENT FILTER

Before selecting a topic, ask:

Is there a real question?

Is the answer worth understanding?

Is there something hidden underneath?

Is there a misconception?

Is there a contradiction?

Is the answer surprising?

Can the idea be explained clearly?

Can evidence support the explanation?

Does the subject have visual potential?

Would the viewer likely think:

"I never thought about it that way."

If the answer is mostly no, the topic should not be prioritized.

# 10. SHORTS CONTENT ARCHITECTURE

Default Shorts structure:

HOOK
→ QUESTION
→ EXPECTATION
→ REVEAL
→ MECHANISM
→ PAYOFF

Not every Short must use every stage explicitly.

The model exists to maintain narrative logic.

---

# 11. HOOK STANDARD

The first seconds must create a reason to continue watching.

The hook should usually introduce:

- an unexpected result;
- a strange observation;
- a contradiction;
- an impossible-looking situation;
- a surprising question;
- a strong curiosity gap.

The hook must connect directly to the actual content.

Do not create a hook the video cannot pay off.

---

# 12. QUESTION STANDARD

The central question should become clear quickly.

Examples:

Why does this happen?

How can this work?

Where does this go?

Why is this designed like that?

What happens if this changes?

Is the common explanation actually correct?

The question is the narrative center of the video.

---

# 13. EXPECTATION STAGE

Where useful, establish what a normal person would expect.

Examples:

Most people assume X.

It looks like X should happen.

You would probably expect X.

But that intuition is incomplete.

The purpose is to create contrast between:

EXPECTED REALITY

and

ACTUAL REALITY

Do not include this stage when it adds unnecessary length.

---

# 14. REVEAL

The reveal introduces the surprising or less-obvious truth.

The reveal should be supported by evidence.

Do not use unsupported statements simply because they sound dramatic.

A reveal should create:

"Oh."

or:

"Wait, really?"

or:

"That makes sense now."

---

# 15. MECHANISM

The mechanism is the actual explanation.

The channel should prioritize understanding over fact dumping.

Explain:

what happens;

why it happens;

how it happens;

what is happening underneath.

Technical detail should be included when it materially improves understanding.

Unnecessary technical detail should be removed.

---

# 16. PAYOFF

The ending should answer the original question.

A good ending creates closure.

The viewer should understand:

What actually happened.

Why it happened.

Why the original observation made sense.

A video may also end with a bigger implication or follow-up question when appropriate.

# 18. TITLE SYSTEM

Titles must represent the real central mystery.

Useful formats include:

Why Does X Happen?

How Does X Actually Work?

What Happens If X?

Where Does X Go?

Why Is X Like This?

The Real Reason X Happens

What You Think About X Is Wrong

How X Is Actually Possible

What Is Really Happening When X?

These are patterns, not mandatory formulas.

Avoid repeating one title structure excessively.

---

# 19. THUMBNAIL SYSTEM

Curious Reality uses:

CONCEPTUAL SHOCK

Core rule:

Do not explain the answer.

Create the need to understand the answer.

Use:

- one dominant idea;
- unusual relationships;
- visual contradiction;
- strong conceptual composition;
- controlled lighting;
- minimal text or no text;
- memorable imagery.

Avoid:

- clutter;
- generic stock imagery;
- unnecessary arrows;
- excessive text;
- forced human reactions;
- repetitive thumbnail templates.

---

# 20. VISUAL STORYTELLING

Visuals should not merely illustrate narration.

Whenever possible, visuals should help the viewer understand the mechanism.

Preferred visual roles:

SHOW
Explain visually.

CONTRAST
Show expected vs actual.

ZOOM
Reveal hidden detail.

TRANSFORM
Show change.

COMPARE
Show differences.

SIMULATE
Represent invisible mechanisms.

MAP
Show relationships or systems.

RECONSTRUCT
Visualize history or inaccessible events when appropriate.

The visual concept should emerge from the underlying question.

---

# 21. AUDIO / NARRATION

Narration should feel:

- human;
- clear;
- controlled;
- confident;
- natural;
- understandable.

Pacing should follow the complexity of the idea.

Use silence when silence improves emphasis.

Music should support the subject.

Sound effects should have purpose.

Do not turn every piece into exaggerated cinematic drama.

---

# 22. RESEARCH REQUIREMENT

Important videos begin with research.

Research workflow:

QUESTION
→ SEARCH
→ PRIMARY SOURCES
→ SECONDARY VERIFICATION
→ CROSS-CHECK
→ RESOLVE CONFLICTS
→ UNDERSTAND
→ WRITE

Preferred source order:

1. Primary documents/data
2. Official sources
3. Peer-reviewed research
4. Authoritative reference material
5. High-quality reporting
6. Strong secondary explainers

# 23. FACTUAL DISCIPLINE

Claims should be internally classified as:

Established Fact

Strong Consensus

Evidence-backed Interpretation

Uncertain / Disputed

Speculation

The video must communicate those distinctions accurately.

Never present:

speculation as fact;

allegation as established truth;

interpretation as certainty;

one disputed claim as settled reality.

# 24. CONTENT SAFETY AND SENSITIVITY

Sensitive subjects must be handled with additional care.

This includes:

- death;
- crime;
- disasters;
- health;
- mental health;
- politics;
- war;
- violence;
- vulnerable people;
- allegations;
- controversial historical events.

Do not sensationalize suffering merely to increase retention.

When evidence is incomplete, say so.

When an allegation is unproven, preserve that distinction.

- Shorts viewed vs swiped;
- likes;
- comments;
- shares;
- subscriber gains;
- traffic sources;
- search terms;
- returning viewers;
