# CURIOUS REALITY — CONTENT RULES

## STATUS

Document Type: Content Creation, Research, Script, Visual, Publishing & Quality Rules
Brand: Curious Reality
Authority: Derived from CURIOUS_REALITY_MASTER_ARCHITECTURE.md

Purpose:

Define the non-negotiable rules for creating, researching, writing, visualizing, reviewing, publishing, and archiving Curious Reality content.

The Master Architecture remains the highest-level source of truth.

---

# 1. CORE CONTENT PRINCIPLE

Curious Reality follows:

QUESTIONS, NOT CATEGORIES.

The subject can change.

The question is the identity.

Every strong piece of content should begin with a question worth understanding.

Core transformation:

CURIOSITY
→ INVESTIGATION
→ UNDERSTANDING
→ EXPLANATION

---

# 2. THE CONTENT PROMISE

Every Curious Reality video should attempt to do at least one of the following:

- reveal a hidden mechanism;
- explain something people experience but do not understand;
- challenge an incomplete assumption;
- answer a genuine question;
- reveal an unexpected contradiction;
- explain how something actually works;
- make an ordinary thing look different;
- investigate an unusual or misunderstood reality.

The content should create understanding, not merely information exposure.

---

# 3. TOPIC SELECTION RULE

Never select a topic simply because:

- it is trending;
- competitors covered it;
- an upload is needed;
- it has high search volume;
- it sounds impressive;
- it is easy to produce.

A topic should be selected because the underlying question is strong.

---

# 4. CURIOUS REALITY TEST

A candidate topic should satisfy at least one:

1. Hidden mechanism
2. Common assumption challenged
3. Genuine viewer question
4. Unexplained everyday experience
5. Unexpected contradiction
6. Ordinary thing seen differently
7. Strong "how is that possible?" moment

If none apply, reject or reframe the topic.

---

# 5. QUESTION-FIRST RULE

Before writing a title or script, define the central question.

The question should be expressible in one clear sentence.

Examples:

Why does this happen?

How does this actually work?

Where does this go?

What is really happening underneath?

What changes if one condition changes?

Is the common explanation actually correct?

The entire piece should remain connected to this central question.

---

# 6. KNOWLEDGE PAYOFF

Every video must contain a meaningful knowledge payoff.

By the end, the viewer should understand something they did not understand before.

A video should not merely say:

"Here is an interesting fact."

It should explain:

What happens.

Why it happens.

How it happens.

What people commonly misunderstand.

Where appropriate, what would happen under different conditions.

---

# 7. RESEARCH BEFORE SCRIPT

Important content must begin with research.

Never write the final script first and search for supporting evidence afterward.

Preferred workflow:

QUESTION
→ SEARCH
→ PRIMARY SOURCES
→ SECONDARY VERIFICATION
→ CROSS-CHECK
→ RESOLVE CONFLICTS
→ UNDERSTAND
→ NOTES
→ SCRIPT

---

# 8. SOURCE HIERARCHY

Preferred source priority:

1. Primary documents and data
2. Official institutions
3. Peer-reviewed research
4. Authoritative books and reference materials
5. High-quality reporting
6. Strong secondary explainers

Use the strongest appropriate source available for the specific claim.

A source should be judged for relevance, authority, recency where relevant, and direct support.

---

# 9. SOURCE DISCIPLINE

Never:

- invent sources;
- invent citations;
- fabricate quotations;
- fabricate statistics;
- attribute claims to sources that do not support them;
- present a source as primary when it is secondary;
- use a weak source to support a strong claim without qualification.

The source must actually support the statement being made.

---

# 10. EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION

Internally classify major claims as:

ESTABLISHED FACT

STRONG CONSENSUS

EVIDENCE-BACKED INTERPRETATION

UNCERTAIN / DISPUTED

SPECULATION

Never intentionally blur these categories.

If something is uncertain, communicate the uncertainty.

If something is disputed, present it as disputed.

If something is speculation, do not present it as established reality.

---

# 11. CONFLICTING EVIDENCE

When credible sources disagree:

1. Identify the disagreement.
2. Compare the sources.
3. Examine evidence quality.
4. Determine what can actually be established.
5. Preserve important uncertainty.
6. Avoid artificial certainty.

Do not select the claim that sounds most dramatic.

Select the claim best supported by evidence.

---

# 12. RESEARCH DEPTH

The creator should understand a topic beyond the exact words that appear in the script.

Before finalizing, be able to answer:

What is happening?

Why is it happening?

How does it happen?

What is happening underneath?

What is the common misconception?

What changes if an important condition changes?

If these cannot be answered where relevant, research is probably incomplete.

---

# 13. SCRIPT RULE

The script exists to explain the answer clearly.

Every line should perform at least one useful function:

CREATE CURIOSITY

EXPLAIN

CLARIFY

PROVE

CONNECT

PAY OFF

If a line performs none of these functions, remove it.

---

# 14. SCRIPT STYLE

English should be:

- natural spoken English;
- simple but not childish;
- intelligent but not unnecessarily academic;
- concise;
- clear;
- direct;
- low-filler;
- believable.

Avoid writing that sounds like a textbook when spoken aloud.

Avoid fake sophistication.

Avoid unnecessary technical jargon.

Use technical terminology when it is necessary for accurate explanation.

---

# 15. BANGLA LOCALIZATION RULE

Bangla is a localization, not a mechanical translation.

Preserve:

- factual meaning;
- curiosity;
- narrative intent;
- emotional context;
- clarity.

Natural Bangla is more important than word-for-word translation.

Do not introduce factual changes merely to make a sentence sound more dramatic.

---

# 16. HOOK RULE

The hook should create a legitimate reason to continue watching.

Possible hook mechanisms:

- surprising result;
- contradiction;
- strange observation;
- impossible-looking event;
- unexpected question;
- counterintuitive reality;
- hidden mechanism.

The hook must connect directly to the actual content.

Never promise a payoff the video cannot deliver.

---

# 17. INFORMATION-GAP RULE

Curiosity can be created by withholding the explanation temporarily.

However:

Withhold the answer for narrative timing.

Do not withhold the truth through deception.

The viewer should think:

"I need to understand this."

not:

"They lied to me to make me click."

---

# 18. EXPECTATION VS REALITY

When appropriate, establish the intuitive expectation first.

Examples:

Most people assume X.

It looks like X should happen.

You would expect X.

Then reveal:

But the actual process is Y.

This contrast is useful when it improves understanding.

Do not include expectation framing when it only adds length.

---

# 19. REVEAL RULE

The reveal must be earned by the evidence and explanation.

Avoid:

- unsupported twists;
- fake secrets;
- exaggerated claims;
- manufactured conspiracy;
- false mystery.

The strongest reveal is often the real mechanism itself.

---

# 20. EXPLANATION RULE

Prefer:

Simple explanation
+
Correct mechanism
+
Useful detail

over:

Large quantity of facts.

The viewer should understand how the pieces connect.

Use examples, analogies, demonstrations, diagrams, comparisons, or simulations when they genuinely improve understanding.

---

# 21. TECHNICAL CONTENT RULE

Technical complexity is allowed.

Unnecessary complexity is not.

When explaining technical subjects:

1. Start with the intuitive idea.
2. Introduce the mechanism.
3. Define necessary technical terms.
4. Explain the important relationship.
5. Return to the original question.

Do not remove necessary technical detail merely to make the content sound simple.

Do not add complexity merely to appear intelligent.

---

# 22. SCIENTIFIC CONTENT RULE

Scientific claims should be proportionate to evidence.

Distinguish:

Observed result

Measured result

Established explanation

Current scientific interpretation

Hypothesis

Speculation

Never present an unproven explanation as settled science.

---

# 23. HISTORICAL CONTENT RULE

Historical content should preserve:

- chronology;
- evidence;
- context;
- uncertainty;
- source limitations.

Avoid compressing complicated historical events into misleading simplistic narratives.

Where historical interpretation is disputed, identify the disagreement.

---

# 24. PSYCHOLOGY / HUMAN BEHAVIOR RULE

Do not turn complex human behavior into simplistic universal statements.

Avoid:

"All people do this because..."

"Humans are programmed to..."

unless the evidence genuinely supports such a claim.

Prefer qualified language when appropriate.

---

# 25. SENSITIVE CONTENT RULE

For subjects involving:

- death;
- crime;
- violence;
- disasters;
- health;
- mental health;
- politics;
- war;
- vulnerable people;
- allegations;
- controversial events;

prioritize:

Accuracy
+
Respect
+
Context
+
Evidence

Never sensationalize suffering merely to improve retention.

Do not turn allegations into facts.

Do not manufacture emotional drama around real-world harm.

---

# 26. MISCONCEPTION RULE

When correcting a misconception:

1. State what people commonly assume, when relevant.
2. Explain why that assumption seems reasonable.
3. Show what is actually happening.
4. Explain the evidence.
5. Close the original question.

Do not mock the audience for believing an intuitive explanation.

The goal is understanding, not superiority.

---

# 27. TITLE RULES

Titles must:

- represent the real topic;
- create curiosity;
- match the actual payoff;
- remain honest;
- avoid unnecessary keyword stuffing.

Do not create a title that implies something the video does not prove.

Useful structures include:

Why Does X Happen?

How Does X Actually Work?

What Happens If X?

Where Does X Go?

Why Is X Like This?

The Real Reason X Happens

What You Think About X Is Wrong

How X Is Actually Possible

What Is Really Happening When X?

These are patterns, not rigid templates.

---

# 28. THUMBNAIL RULES

Core concept:

CONCEPTUAL SHOCK

The thumbnail should create the need to understand the answer.

Preferred:

- one dominant idea;
- visual contradiction;
- unusual composition;
- strong conceptual relationship;
- minimal text;
- memorable imagery;
- clear subject hierarchy.

Avoid:

- clutter;
- generic stock composition;
- unnecessary arrows;
- excessive circles;
- excessive text;
- fake reaction expressions;
- misleading imagery.

---

# 29. THUMBNAIL ANSWER-REVEAL RULE

Do not completely explain the answer in the thumbnail.

The thumbnail should establish:

"What is going on here?"

not:

"Here is the complete explanation."

The title and thumbnail should work together without duplicating each other.

---

# 30. VISUAL RULE

Visuals must serve meaning.

Preferred functions include:

SHOW

COMPARE

EXPLAIN

TRANSFORM

REVEAL

SIMULATE

MAP

RECONSTRUCT

CONTEXTUALIZE

Visual beauty is valuable.

Visual meaning is mandatory.

Priority:

Meaning
→ Clarity
→ Curiosity
→ Beauty

---

# 31. VISUAL ORIGINALITY

Do not simply reproduce the visual style of another creator.

Original visual identity should come from:

- interpretation;
- composition;
- concept;
- metaphor;
- scale;
- movement;
- visual explanation.

The goal is to make the audience recognize a Curious Reality visual language.

---

# 32. AI CONTENT RULE

AI may assist with:

- research discovery;
- organization;
- outlining;
- drafting;
- localization;
- visual ideation;
- editing assistance;
- SEO;
- metadata;
- archive management.

AI output is never automatically the truth.

When accuracy matters:

AI OUTPUT
→ VERIFY
→ FINAL CLAIM

Never:

AI OUTPUT
→ PUBLISH AS FACT

---

# 33. AI HALLUCINATION RULE

The AI must never invent:

- statistics;
- sources;
- quotations;
- study results;
- historical events;
- technical specifications;
- names;
- dates;
- evidence.

When information is missing:

ASK

or

STATE UNCERTAINTY.

Do not fill gaps with plausible-sounding fiction.

---

# 34. FACT-CHECKING RULE

Before publication, re-check all major claims.

Pay particular attention to:

- numbers;
- dates;
- names;
- causal statements;
- scientific mechanisms;
- technical specifications;
- legal claims;
- medical claims;
- allegations;
- direct quotations.

The more consequential the claim, the stronger the verification requirement.

---

# 35. QUOTE RULE

Use quotations only when:

- the wording is important;
- the source is identifiable;
- the quote can be verified;
- quotation is preferable to paraphrase.

Never invent a quote.

Never alter a quote in a way that changes its meaning.

---

# 36. STATISTICS RULE

Whenever a statistic is important:

- know the source;
- know what population it refers to;
- know the time period;
- understand the measurement;
- avoid implying more precision than the evidence supports.

Do not use a statistic merely because it sounds impressive.

---

# 37. CAUSATION RULE

Do not confuse:

Correlation

with:

Causation.

When evidence only demonstrates association, describe association.

Only claim causality when evidence supports it.

---

# 38. UNCERTAINTY RULE

Uncertainty is not a weakness.

When uncertainty matters, communicate it.

Useful language may include:

"The evidence suggests..."

"Researchers are still debating..."

"The exact cause remains uncertain..."

"Current evidence supports..."

"One interpretation is..."

Do not convert uncertainty into certainty for dramatic effect.

---

# 39. CONTENT LENGTH RULE

Content length must follow the information requirement.

Shorts:

Compact idea
+
Clear explanation
+
Payoff

Long-form:

Depth
+
Context
+
Evidence
+
Connected explanation

Never stretch content just to increase duration.

Never compress important information purely to save seconds.

---

# 40. ENDING RULE

The ending should close the loop opened by the beginning.

The viewer should leave knowing:

What happened.

Why it happened.

What was misunderstood.

Where appropriate, what the bigger implication is.

A CTA is optional.

Understanding is mandatory.

---

# 41. PUBLISHING QUALITY GATE

Before publication verify:

## Knowledge

Is the central claim correct?

## Sources

Are important claims adequately supported?

## Script

Does the script answer the central question?

## Hook

Does the opening create legitimate curiosity?

## Visuals

Do visuals improve understanding?

## Audio

Is narration clear?

## Title

Is the title accurate and compelling?

## Thumbnail

Does the thumbnail create curiosity without misleading?

## Ending

Does the content pay off the original question?

If an important area fails:

RETURN TO PRODUCTION.

---

# 42. PUBLISHING STATUS

Use clear content states:

IDEA

SHORTLISTED

RESEARCHING

VERIFIED

SCRIPTED

VISUAL PLAN

EDITING

REVIEW

SCHEDULED

PUBLISHED

ANALYZED

ARCHIVED

Do not mark a video complete simply because it has been uploaded.

---

# 43. ARCHIVE RULE

Every important published topic should become a reusable knowledge asset.

Archive at least:

- video ID;
- title;
- core question;
- format;
- publication date;
- YouTube URL;
- website URL;
- summary;
- sources;
- keywords;
- related questions;
- related videos;
- status;
- performance notes.

Where appropriate, also preserve:

- script;
- thumbnail concept;
- visual concept;
- future expansion ideas.

---

# 44. REUSE RULE

Strong research should be reusable.

One research package may produce:

1. Short
2. Bangla version
3. Website article
4. Diagram
5. Source collection
6. Related question
7. Long-form expansion
8. Follow-up Short
9. Searchable archive record

Do not discard useful research after one publication.

---

# 45. WEBSITE CONTENT RULE

The website must not simply duplicate the YouTube description.

A video page should provide additional value where appropriate.

Possible additions:

- deeper explanation;
- mechanisms;
- context;
- research notes;
- source list;
- related questions;
- related videos;
- diagrams;
- additional details.

The website should function as a knowledge layer.

---

# 46. SEO CONTENT RULE

SEO must serve the user and the content.

Use relevant:

- titles;
- descriptions;
- headings;
- terminology;
- internal links;
- structured data;
- page relationships.

Do not use irrelevant keyword stuffing.

Do not write content solely for search engines.

The actual question and useful explanation must remain central.

---

# 47. INTERNAL LINKING RULE

Where relevant, connect:

Video
→ Related Video

Video
→ Related Question

Video
→ Topic

Article
→ Related Article

Article
→ Video

Question
→ Related Questions

The archive should gradually become an interconnected knowledge system rather than isolated pages.

---

# 48. COMMUNITY RULE

Useful audience comments may become research inputs.

Look for:

- genuine questions;
- misconceptions;
- corrections;
- alternative examples;
- requests for deeper explanation;
- unresolved confusion.

Do not manufacture audience questions.

Use genuine community signals.

---

# 49. CORRECTION RULE

When a meaningful factual error is discovered:

1. Verify the issue.
2. Determine severity.
3. Correct public content where practical.
4. Update the website record.
5. Update sources if required.
6. Communicate the correction when appropriate.

Trust is more important than pretending the brand never makes mistakes.

---

# 50. ORIGINALITY RULE

Topic overlap with other creators is acceptable.

Content copying is not.

Originality should come from:

Question
+
Research
+
Framing
+
Explanation
+
Visual Concept
+
Storytelling

Never use another creator's content as a substitute for original thinking.

---

# 51. PERFORMANCE RULE

Analytics matter.

But performance does not override truth.

Primary performance lessons should focus on:

- question strength;
- hook effectiveness;
- retention;
- visual clarity;
- title curiosity;
- thumbnail clarity;
- audience response;
- long-term searchability.

Do not sacrifice credibility for one performance spike.

---

# 52. CONTENT DECISION HIERARCHY

When content priorities conflict:

TRUTH
→ UNDERSTANDING
→ VIEWER VALUE
→ CURIOSITY
→ STORY
→ VISUAL QUALITY
→ SPEED

This order is non-negotiable.

---

# 53. RED FLAGS

Immediately reconsider content containing:

- unsupported extraordinary claims;
- fabricated authority;
- invented studies;
- fake quotations;
- misleading thumbnails;
- false urgency;
- fake secrecy;
- conspiracy presented as fact;
- exaggerated medical claims;
- unsupported causal claims;
- copied scripts;
- excessive filler;
- empty viral phrasing.

---

# 54. FINAL CONTENT TEST

Before publishing, ask:

Would I still publish this if views did not matter?

Does this answer a real question?

Did we research it properly?

Do we understand the mechanism?

Can we support the important claims?

Does the title accurately represent the content?

Does the thumbnail create honest curiosity?

Does the viewer leave with better understanding?

Will this still be useful years later?

If the answer is no in an important area:

Fix the content first.

---

# 55. THE GOLDEN RULE

Do not simply give the answer.

Make the viewer understand why the answer is the answer.

---

# 56. FINAL CONTENT PRINCIPLE

Curious Reality content should transform:

A question

into:

Understanding.

The final viewer experience should be:

"I noticed something."

→

"Why does that happen?"

→

"I want to know."

→

"Now I understand."

→

"I never looked at it that way before."
